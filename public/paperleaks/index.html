India's exam paper leak crisis, in data

What happens afterwards

Cancellation is the most common response — but a third of all cases end with no judgement at all: the claim is dismissed, denied, or simply produces no recorded action. Only 15 of 120 incidents led to a re-exam being held or ordered.

Methodology

Base data: India Question Paper Leaks — Kaggle (66 incidents, Jun 2014–Aug 2024, CC-BY-NC-SA-4.0), with state-name typos cleaned. 54 further incidents (Jan 2024 – Jul 2026) were researched from credible outlets — The Indian Express, The Hindu, ThePrint/PTI, Deccan Herald, LiveLaw, Careers360, India TV, regional dailies and official board advisories — each row carrying its citation. "Accused" incidents include officially denied fake-leak scams, kept deliberately: they are part of the leak economy. Multi-day episodes of a single leak are counted once. Candidate counts are as reported (registered or appeared) and exist for 17 incidents; totals are therefore minimums.
